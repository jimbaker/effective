# A claim is not evidence

The house posture, and every line of it was bought.

## A ratification is a claim

Test a ratified recommendation (an author's, a reviewer's, or an outside model's) **before
executing it**. Of five tested rather than executed, **four changed the answer**, and the fifth
changed the stated reasoning while its conclusion held. A naming decision reversed and reframed; a type design
that would have bought a rename; a fix whose named landing site did not exist in `src/`; a plan
predicting three reachable outcomes where two are.

A **plan's premises** are claims too, and so is the integer a plan quotes. Of one plan's three
premises, two were refuted within the hour.

## Derive the expected value first, then run

Write down what the design says the answer should be **before** measuring, and settle open
questions by measuring rather than by plausible inference. Knowing the rule does not fire it: route
the claim to something that **runs**.

## An instrument is a claim

And **the one you built this hour is the one you are least able to check.** Five defects in one
arc, four of them in instruments built the same day; every one found by an outside reader or by
moving the thing, none by running it. An instrument that is green and wrong is invisible to running
it: you find it by MOVING what it describes.

See [concepts/enforcer-domain](enforcer-domain.md) for the shape this takes in a gate.

## A green test can be weaker than it looks

**The SQLite engine is at parity with Absurd on event delivery**, and where the two disagreed the
embedded engine conformed; Absurd is the reference for durability. What survives is a rule about
evidence rather than about engines. The dev engine's `events(task_id, name)` key was an unrecorded
day-one deviation, and its **extra precision supplied an isolation production did not have**: a
two-generation respawn chain under one `govern` gate charged $5,000,000 on a $5 approval without
parking, measured twice on real Absurd, while the SQLite test stayed green.

That hole is closed: `govern:` and `approve:` carry a generation coordinate and delivery is
broadcast on both engines, so the property lives in the **name** rather than in one engine's
addressing. The caution is not closed: **an engine more precise than the reference can launder an
unproved property into a green test.** Ask what a passing test *relies on*, not just that it passes.

Two more shapes of the same thing. **A stub is not the system**: reading an annotation is not
reproducing, and a `TypedDict` or a `.pyi` is a claim *about* a value, never the value. And
**coverage can be structurally incapable** of seeing a defect: a function that had never been
executed was 4 statements of 44, so its module read 90.9%, above a per-file 90% gate.

## Nominal where structural belonged

Every defect one arc found was a **nominal** thing standing where a **structural** one belonged: a
denylist for an arm term, `startswith` for a parse, a payload string for a scope frame, a tool-name
prefix for an arg shape, a hand-spelled key for a minter's call.

Its working corollary: **a passage is validated by its CLAIM, not its spelling.** The check is
never "does this parse" but "run what this sentence asserts".

## Let something other than the author choose the cases

An author cannot self-check a **premise**, nor **rule compliance**. Measured n=4:
every instance in a class that was written down at the time and every one caught by an external
reader. **The moment you are most confident is the moment you are applying a rule from memory.**

So: falsify rather than confirm, reproduce rather than read, and do not scope a review to "the
risky part": the interesting parts have already been reviewed.
